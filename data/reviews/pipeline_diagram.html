Bird Translation AI · architecture

Classifying a bird call

A frozen BirdNET backbone does the listening. One small trainable projection head does the deciding. Or: a know-it-all who won't learn, and the one bit that did the homework.

frozen — pretrained, used unmodified trainable — built for this project training only

Glossary, honestly

FROZEN BACKBONEA model someone else trained, that we are not allowed to touch and would only ruin.
EMBEDDINGA sound rewritten as 1,024 numbers, none of which mean anything on their own.
TEMPORAL SEGMENTATIONThrowing away the wind, the traffic, and the forty seconds where nothing happened.
PROJECTION HEADA small network whose entire job is to make similar things sit near each other. It is the whole contribution.
SUPERVISED CONTRASTIVE LOSSShown thousands of pairs and told "same" or "different" until it stopped guessing.
CLASS PROTOTYPEThe average of every example of one call type. A platonic ideal of a robin having a bad day.
NEAREST-CENTROID CLASSIFICATIONMeasure which blob it's closest to. Yes, that's it.
GROUPED CROSS-VALIDATIONMaking sure the exam questions weren't in the textbook.

What is borrowed, what is not

BirdNET — from the Cornell Lab of Ornithology and Chemnitz University of Technology — does both the species identification and the feature extraction, and was used entirely unmodified. Everything after it was built for this project: the projection head, the class prototypes, and the labelled dataset they were fitted to. Without the trained head the system is right about seven times in ten; with it, eight.

The honest caveat: none of this would run without BirdNET, which somebody else trained at real expense. The saving isn't in making things small — it's in not training from scratch when the hard part is already done.
